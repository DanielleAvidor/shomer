# Interview 05: a man aged 81 (outside the family)

Date: 2026-11-27. Not related to the builder. Notes are the builder's summary; no recording, no verbatim quotes, no name kept.

## What he said (as reported)
- He sometimes taps links he should not tap and is not really aware of the issue.
- He has already been the victim of a scam three times.
- Recently he started sending messages that look suspicious to him to his son. Most of those turn out to be ordinary messages.
- Recently he missed a message that was in fact a scam, and fell for it.

## What it shows
- **Forwarding to family has two costs.** Most of what he sends is harmless, so the son carries the load, and the one real scam still got through because he did not flag it. A person's own sense of "suspicious" is not a reliable filter.
- It supports the earlier pattern that the person who is harmed is often the one who does not suspect (see interview 04).
- It is a partial real-world example of the hypothesis about effort and missed messages: effort goes to the wrong messages. It is still one man, reported by me, and he was not asked about it directly.

## Limits
- One person, reported by the builder; no details on what each scam was, how much was lost, or on which channel.
- Older adult self-reports of past events can be incomplete.
- He may or may not have also answered the anonymous survey; I cannot tell.
