# Interview 04: a friend's grandmother (77)

Date: 2026-11-27. Not a relative of the builder, so this is the first person outside the family. Notes are the builder's summary; no recording, no verbatim quotes.

## What she said
- In the recent past she tapped two phishing links and once also entered her credit card details.
- She did not know the messages were fake, so she did not ask anyone before acting.
- When something looks suspicious to her, she sends it to her granddaughter.
- The difficulty: she often cannot tell, because the messages usually imitate services she already uses regularly, such as Israel Post.

## What it changes
- It is the first case of real harm (card details entered), and the failure was not carelessness: she had no reason to doubt the message, so asking family never happened. A product that waits to be asked would not have helped her; the phone has to speak first.
- "Ask family" works only after she already suspects. Her trigger for suspicion is the problem.
- Imitating a service she already uses is the core pattern: the message looks routine because it is routine. This supports the context question ("were you expecting this from Israel Post?") and the warning that names the organisation.

## Limits
- One person, reported by the builder, not independent of the builder's circle.
- No numbers: how often, which messages, how much money. Not recorded.
- Self-report from memory, from an older adult: details may be incomplete.
