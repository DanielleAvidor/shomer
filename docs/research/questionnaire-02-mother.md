# Oral questionnaire 02: mother (in person, one participant)

**Date:** 2026-11-21. Same format and limits as `interview-01-grandmother.md`: questions prepared by Claude, read aloud by the builder (her daughter), answers typed by the builder, paraphrased. Pilot, not a survey sample.

| Topic | Answer |
|---|---|
| Phone | iPhone. Receives SMS, email and WhatsApp. |
| Suspicious messages in the last six months | Road 6 and Israel Post, both by SMS (the same two scams her mother received). |
| What she did | Asked the family WhatsApp group whether anyone was expecting a delivery, and sent the message to an AI chat to check. |
| What would help most | The phone tells her immediately: "don't click, the link does not lead to the real site." Sharing or pasting a message into an app is what she wants and says would help her. |
| Alert to a family member about herself | Not needed. |
| Alert about her own mother (grandmother) | She would like to be alerted, only when the danger is real and certain. The grandmother said she is fine with that. |

## What this suggests (hypotheses)
1. **Same pattern as the grandmother:** ask the family, then ask a chatbot. The context question ("is anyone expecting a delivery?") is something she already does by herself.
2. **A guardian role exists, but narrowly:** she wants to be told about her mother only when the scam is certain, and only with her mother's agreement.
3. **iPhone changes the technical plan.** An iPhone app cannot read incoming SMS the way Android can. Paste and share are what is realistic for her; a native iPhone share extension is a later step.

## Limits
One participant, same family as interview 01, the builder was present, paraphrased answers. The grandmother's agreement to alerts was given aloud in a family setting; a real product needs her explicit consent on screen.
