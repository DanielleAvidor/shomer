# Oral questionnaire 01: grandmother (in person, one participant)

**Date:** 2026-11-21. **Format:** short structured oral questionnaire (about 8 questions, the same ones planned for the written survey). Questions were prepared by Claude and read aloud by the builder (her granddaughter), who typed the answers. About 10 minutes. This is one participant, so it is a pilot, not a survey sample.
**Participant:** one older woman, uses an Android phone, receives both SMS and WhatsApp. Name withheld.

## What she said (paraphrased by the interviewer)
| Topic | Answer |
|---|---|
| Phone | Android, ordinary use, SMS and WhatsApp |
| Suspicious messages in the last six months | Three: a Road 6 (toll) payment, a refund ("זיכוי"), and Israel Post. All had suspicious links. |
| What she did | Sent them to her granddaughter and asked. The granddaughter pasted the link into a general-purpose AI chat, which said the link looked suspicious and did not lead to the real site. |
| If the granddaughter had not been available | She might have clicked by mistake because she would not have known it was suspicious. |
| Has she clicked a scam link before? | Yes, in the past. This time she was saved by what she already knew: she had no parcel coming from Israel Post and had not used Road 6. |
| What would help most | The phone checks the link by itself and warns her: suspicious link, does not lead to the real site, this is a scam. |
| Which warning wording is clearest | "אל תלחצי על הקישור. הוא לא מוביל לאתר של דואר ישראל." (names the organisation, says what to do) |
| Where she wants the warning | A pop-up on the screen. |
| Alert to a family member? | "Not needed, she manages." |
| Idea of the product | It should replace the need to ask family: she feels safe and knows what to open and what not. |

## What this suggests (hypotheses, not conclusions)
1. **The current solution is a manual version of the product:** ask a family member, who checks with a chatbot. Competitors are "asking family" and general AI chat, not other apps.
2. **Her own context knowledge is the strongest defence** ("I have no parcel"). A useful warning explains why, so it supports her judgement and does not replace it. A context question ("were you expecting a parcel?") is worth testing.
3. **She wants independence, not a guardian.** She declined automatic alerts to family. That challenges the family-screen premise in the first PRD. A candidate design: automatic check and pop-up for her; sharing with family only when she chooses ("not sure, send to someone to check").
4. **She wants a clear verdict.** The engine often says "can't verify". That tension needs design work.

## Limits (important)
- One participant, so none of this generalises.
- The interviewer is her granddaughter and was present, which can make answers more polite or more dependent.
- Answers are paraphrased, not recorded.
- The questions were not neutral in all cases (some offered options). The wording preference was chosen between two options written by Claude.
- Needs repeating with the mother and at least two other older adults, ideally with someone who is not family.
