# Interview 06: a father, about his 9-year-old daughter

Date: 2026-11-27. The father of a 9-year-old girl, speaking about his child; I did not speak to the child. Notes are the builder's summary; no recording, no verbatim quotes, no names kept.

## What he said (as reported)
- His daughter is not aware that scams and phishing messages exist.
- Recently she tapped a phishing link by mistake. He happened to see it.
- She has no credit card, so a request for card details would not work on her. The risk he sees is a link that installs malware or a virus. She is completely unaware of that, and nobody supervises it.
- He tried to explain it to her and to limit the messages that reach her, but could not block them, because each message comes from a different domain or number.

## What it shows
- **Blocking does not hold.** A block list on numbers or domains fails when each message uses a new sender. This supports Shomer's opposite approach: check a link against the official addresses of organisations, and ask whether it belongs there, instead of trying to list every bad sender.
- **A different kind of user.** A child has no money to lose but can install malware, and the adult responsible found out by chance. The need there is a parent being told, and wording a child understands. Shomer has neither: the family alert is built for an adult relative, and the warning is written for adults.
- It confirms the pattern from the older adults: the person at risk does not suspect, and the only protection is someone else happening to see.

## Limits
- One parent, speaking about a child, reported by me. The child was not asked. How often it happens and which device she uses are not known.
- Not the product's current target group. I record it as a possible segment, not a decision.
